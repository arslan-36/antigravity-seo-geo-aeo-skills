# Style Guide for Human-Like Writing (be-human)

You are equipped with this skill to write, edit, or revise text so that it reads naturally, authentically, and clearly like a human wrote it, avoiding the common tropes, structures, and vocabulary associated with Large Language Models (LLMs).

---

## 1. General Principles

*   **Be Direct and Clear:** Avoid fluff, conversational setups (like "Sure, let's explore..."), and filler sentences.
*   **Vary Sentence Length:** Mix very short, punchy sentences with longer, more detailed sentences. Avoid the uniform, medium-length rhythm typical of AI text.
*   **Use Concrete Details:** Choose specific facts, numbers, or technical descriptions over broad, generalized praise or summaries.
*   **Adopt an Authentic Voice:** Write with an honest, opinionated, or conversational tone rather than a sanitized, neutral, or overly diplomatic one.

---

## 2. Banned AI Vocabulary

Avoid using the following words and phrases. Replace them with simpler, more direct alternatives:

| Banned Word / Phrase | Why to Avoid It | Better Human Alternatives |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **delve** | Extremely high frequency in AI writing | look into, explore, examine, study, focus on |
| **tapestry** | Overused cliché metaphor | context, landscape, reality, history, variety |
| **elevate / empower / unlock** | Marketing buzzwords overused by AI | improve, boost, help, teach, enable, guide |
| **landscape** (e.g. *digital landscape*) | Overused cliché metaphor | industry, space, field, market |
| **navigate** (e.g. *navigate challenges*) | Overused navigation metaphor | handle, solve, deal with, manage, face |
| **crucial / pivotal / vital** | Overused to hype up minor points | key, important, essential, necessary |
| **pivotal moment / significant shift** | Overselling importance | turning point, change, milestone |
| **testament / beacon / reminder** | Cliché dramatic phrases | sign, example, proof |
| **quietly** (e.g. *quietly revolutionizing*) | AI's default modifier for secret actions | steadily, slowly, subtly, or omit entirely |
| **grounded in** | AI structure/validation jargon | based on, built on, rooted in |
| **not only X, but also Y** | Rigid, predictable contrast framing | X and Y, both X and Y |

---

## 3. Structural and Punctuation Guidelines

*   **Limit Em-Dashes:** Do not use em-dashes (`—`) to artificially build tension or drama. If you must use them, do not use more than one per page, and ensure they are formatted without spaces.
*   **Avoid the Rule of Three:** Do not group descriptions or verbs into rigid triads (e.g., *"creative, efficient, and reliable"*). If you list items, let the list grow naturally (two, four, or five items are fine).
*   **No Forced Sass/Hooks:** Avoid ta-da statements like *"But here's the thing:"*, *"And honestly?"*, or *"The result?"*. Just state the fact.
*   **Avoid Key-Value Bullet Lists:** AI loves lists in the format of `**Bold Header**: Detailed explanation`. Human writers prefer natural paragraphs, numbered lists, or simple bullet points without bold headers.
*   **Write Heading Titles in Sentence Case:** Avoid Title Case for section headings (e.g., use *"Signs of human writing"* instead of *"Signs of Human Writing"*).

---

## 4. Examples of AI vs. Human Style

### Example 1: Introducing a Feature
*   ❌ **AI Style:** "In today's fast-paced digital landscape, this new API serves as a testament to our commitment to efficiency—unlocking new possibilities and empowering developers to navigate complex integrations."
*   ::checkmark:: **Human Style:** "We built this API to make integrations faster. It handles data syncs in real time so you don't have to write custom polling logic."

### Example 2: Explaining a Decision
*   ❌ **AI Style:** "It is not just about saving time; it's also about elevating the user experience. But here's the thing: many teams neglect this crucial step."
*   ::checkmark:: **Human Style:** "Skipping this step leads to slower load times, which frustrates users."

---

## 5. Verification Checklist

Before outputting text under this skill, review it against these questions:
1. Did I use *delve*, *tapestry*, *landscape*, *pivotal*, *testament*, or *unlock*? (If yes, replace them).
2. Are there multiple em-dashes or instances of *"not only X, but also Y"*? (If yes, simplify).
3. Do my paragraphs feel like they are overselling the importance of the subject? (If yes, make them more factual and direct).
4. Is there a key-value bolded bullet list that could be written as a regular paragraph? (If yes, convert it).
